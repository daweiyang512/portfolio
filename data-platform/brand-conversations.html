Brand conversations and engagement

A reproducible check of a small social-post dataset and its supplied informational/emotional labels, followed by descriptive comparisons across four brands.

Data and record integrity

The main assignment CSV contains 324 posts from Amazon, PayPal, UPS, and Walmart, dated from 15 December 2022 to 10 April 2023 (UTC). The label file also contains 324 records. The numeric id field has only six distinct values, so it is not a reliable unique key. The reconstruction validates a one-to-one match using UTC timestamp and exact post text before attaching labels.

A separate course update file contains 38 posts from a different set of accounts. It is not part of these results.

Topic distribution

Across the matched sample, 205 posts carry the informational label and 119 carry the emotional label. UPS has the highest emotional-label share in this sample.

Engagement comparison

The script summarizes likes, replies, and retweets by brand and supplied topic label, as well as monthly post counts. These are observed counts, not exposure-adjusted rates. Account size, post reach, and timing can affect the comparisons.

The output supports descriptive questions about how the supplied labels and observed engagement vary in this sample. It does not show that emotional wording causes more engagement.

Method and limits

  • Parse timestamps in UTC and reject duplicate timestamp/text keys.
  • Validate a one-to-one source-to-label join before calculating any summary.
  • Treat supplied topic labels as inputs; the labeling model or rubric is not reproduced here.
  • Summarize topic shares within brand, engagement by brand/topic, and monthly topic counts.
  • Do not generalize this small observational sample to all posts or customers.
